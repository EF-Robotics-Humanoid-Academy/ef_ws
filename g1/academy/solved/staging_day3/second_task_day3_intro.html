Introduction

This task covers teach() / repeat(): record an arm motion by physically guiding the robot's arm with your hands, then play it back automatically. teach(name) makes the arm (and any reachable Dex3 fingers) backdrivable with light damping while the waist stays fixed and stiff, and records continuously until you press Enter. repeat(name) replays the recorded motion smoothly over rt/arm_sdk, blended with AI Sport -- no dev mode involved.

Known caveat: occasionally the arm can feel locked/not freely movable right after teach() is called, if a previous release/engage hasn't fully settled yet. If that happens, re-run release_arms() first and try again -- this is exactly why the last cell in this notebook calls release_arms() a second time, as a safety net, even though it was already called once at the start.

Setup

Connect to the robot over DDS and get a G1 handle.

Task 1 - release_arms() before manual guidance

You cannot safely put your hands on the arm and move it while rt/arm_sdk is driving it at full weight. release_arms(duration_s=3.0, rate_hz=50.0) eases the blend weight to 0 first.

Task 2 - release_fingers() so the hand is backdrivable too

Same idea as Task 1, but for the Dex3 fingers: release_fingers(hand="right", hold_s=0.5, rate_hz=50.0, persistent=True). Note its default is persistent=True -- it keeps re-releasing on a background thread until you call stop_release_fingers(), so the fingers stay loose while you're guiding the arm through a gesture in Task 3.

Task 3 - teach(sequence_name, poll_s=0.02, arm_kd=3.0): record by hand

Records a hand-guided motion: the arm joints are held backdrivable with light damping (kp=0, kd=arm_kd, default 3.0 -- deliberately low and well under the MAX_KD=10.0 ceiling, so the arm stays easy to push around), the waist is held fixed at its start pose with high stiffness, and reachable Dex3 fingers are recorded too. It prints a running sample count and keeps recording until you press Enter. Sequences are persisted to arm_sequences.json, so they survive a kernel restart.

Safety: physically support the arm's weight the whole time you're teaching -- "backdrivable" does not mean "no gravity"; light kd damping alone will not hold an outstretched arm up on its own.

Task 4 - repeat(sequence_name, speed=1.0, rate_hz=50.0, ...): play it back

Ramps safely from the current pose to the recorded sequence's first sample (respecting the same joint-speed safety cap as move_ll_joint, DEFAULT_MAX_JOINT_SPEED_RAD_S = 0.6 rad/s), plays the recording back at speedx, holds the final pose, and replays any recorded Dex3 finger frames for hands that are actually reachable right now.

Safety: stand clear before calling this. Unlike teach() (where you are guiding the arm by hand), repeat() drives the arm on its own, at up to the joint-speed cap above.

Task 5 - release_arms() again: hand back control

Always end a manual-arm session by releasing, even if you believe you already did -- it's a cheap, idempotent safety net against the "arms not movable" caveat mentioned in the introduction.

Safety

Run no command cell until you've read its markdown above, the space around the robot is clear, and you have a way to reach FSM damp (or the physical e-stop) if something looks wrong. Code in this notebook is never invoked automatically -- you decide when each cell runs.
